# Eliminate-Reduce-Raise-Create Grid

**Status:** DRAFT value innovation hypothesis. Prepared with AI support from our existing evidence log and market research. Every row still needs the team's explicit confirmation before Group Project I.

**Target user:** Students aged 19 to 24 in Spain looking for a job or an internship

**Job to Be Done:** Find openings that really fit their profile and interests, so applications turn into interviews and offers instead of hours of searching and mass applying (evidence-log/jtbd.md)

**Strongest current alternative:** The manual routine, which is applying at high volume on LinkedIn and InfoJobs

**Evidence basis for the baseline:** Interview 01 (n=1): hundreds of applications through job websites, some interviews, never employed through one. Supported by published research on application volume (NACE 2025). Limitation: one interview is a signal, not validation. The survey and the 7 planned interviews test whether this baseline holds.

## ERRC Grid

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Eliminate | Sponsored and employer-paid placement in what a student is shown | That job platforms are paid by the side that wants attention, so ranking serves employers | Removes ad sales, ad operations and the revenue that funds them. ## Value innovation hypothesis

We do not compete on how many jobs we can show. We compete on showing only roles that fit and being honest about why, paid by universities rather than by employers buying attention, and we add a forward-looking view of which entry-level roles are disappearing. Lower cost comes from a far smaller, verified catalogue and no ad operation. Higher buyer value comes from accuracy, stated reasons and a view of the future that no current alternative offers.

This remains a hypothesis, not a validated blue ocean or a confirmed business model.

## Critical outstanding validation points

1. How many unsuitable matches a student tolerates before abandoning the tool. This sets the accuracy bar for the whole strategy.
2. Whether anyone other than employers will pay, since we eliminated the standard revenue model. Universities are the proposed payer and have not been approached.
3. Whether the entry-level shift monitor is wanted by students or only interesting to us.
4. Whether a smaller catalogue reads as focused or as empty.

## Evidence boundaries

- Based on real evidence (n=1): trust begins with never showing an unsuitable job; high application volume produces interviews but no hire.
- Based on team and AI inference: the sponsored-placement elimination, the university payer, and the shift monitor. None has been tested with a real student or a university.
- Open contradiction: our Sceptic persona assumed explanations earn trust, while interview 01 named accuracy. Both stay in the grid until the survey and the interviews separate them.
