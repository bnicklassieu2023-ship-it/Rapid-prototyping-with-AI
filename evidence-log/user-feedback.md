## Survey round 1, 7 October 2026: our second real-user source

Four real students, recruited through our own networks, answering about their own behaviour. Full per-question counts, the four verbatim objections and the decision-rule read-out are in [survey.md](survey.md). This section records only what it does to our evidence.

**Status: REAL-USER EVIDENCE (n=4). A second signal, not validation.** The survey is open until 13 October. Under the rule we set in advance, a sample this small is reported as counts and named as weak, never as percentages.

### What it supports

| Claim | Round 1 |
|---|---|
| Trust starts with accuracy, not with explanations | All 4 picked "every job shown actually fits my profile". It was the only option all four chose. Consistent with interview 01 and with our v0.3 riskiest assumption |
| Where a posting came from, and whether the employer is real, matter | 3 of 4 each. Both are what our Raise row verifies, so the Raise row is aimed at things students say they care about |
| The university channel is open | 4 of 4 would use it free through their student login |

### What it contradicts

**Interview 01 said he would stop trusting a site that showed him any unsuitable job. Round 1 says nobody is that strict.** Not one of the four chose 1 or 2 unsuitable suggestions as their limit; two chose 3 to 5 and two chose more than 5.

Both sources are real students. We are not averaging them into a middle number. The contradiction is recorded as a contradiction, and resolving it is now an explicit job for the seven interviews: ask what the limit is, and ask it after the student has seen a real shortlist rather than in the abstract, because a tolerance stated in a survey and a tolerance observed in front of six real job cards are not the same measurement.

Note what this does **not** change: accuracy is still the first thing all four named. The question is how steep the cliff is, not whether accuracy is the thing.

### What it works against

**Our push force.** Interview 01 had sent hundreds of applications and had never been hired. In round 1, two of four sent 5 or fewer applications, and two of four have been hired through a platform. All four are bachelor students and three are 21 or under, so they are earlier in the search than interview 01, which may explain it. It may also mean the Mass Applier is a narrower segment than our opportunity statement assumes. Recorded here as a challenge to be settled by the interviews, not explained away.

**The Mass Applier persona.** One of four shows the pattern. The persona is not withdrawn on four answers, but it can no longer be described as the typical student, and the slide should not imply that it is.

### What it adds that we did not have

Four written objections from real people, quoted in full in survey.md. Three of them are new to our evidence log, and one of them is aimed straight at our idea: employers are beginning to state in job descriptions that they do not accept applications made through agencies, which a respondent raised unprompted as a risk to what we are building.

### Still unknown after round 1

- The trigger that starts a search. Still open, still not asked of anyone.
- Willingness to pay. The payment questions were cut from the short survey, and one respondent answered "money" to the objection question, so it goes to the interviews.
- Whether any of this holds for US students. Every respondent so far is at IE. Our declared proxy assumption is doing a lot of work and the presentation says so.
- Whether a student accepts a shortlist we actually built. That is test run 03 and it has not been run.
