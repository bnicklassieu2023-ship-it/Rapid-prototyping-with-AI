# Test run 02: has this already been done, and if so what differentiates us?

**Status: RUN AND COMPLETE. It changed two things in our plan, one of them badly.**

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Run on | 7 October 2026 |
| Run by | Claude (Cowork, claude-opus-5) as a desk run, instructed and reviewed by Linda |
| Type | Desk run on public sources. No accounts were created and no competitor product was used from the inside |
| Question | The graded feedback asked us to test whether the idea already exists and what would differentiate us. So: does a product already do what we promise, and if not, what exactly is the gap? |
| Method | Take the two moves our ERRC grid rests on, Eliminate employer influence and Raise verification depth, and check each incumbent against those two moves specifically rather than comparing feature lists |

## Why this framing and not a feature comparison

A feature comparison would have told us what we already knew, that every incumbent has search, filters and recommendations. The only question that decides whether our venture has a reason to exist is whether anyone already does the two things we claim as our strategy. So each incumbent was checked against those two, and nothing else.

## Result

| Product | Does it remove employer influence over what a student sees? | Does it verify that a posting is real, still open and explicit on work authorisation? | Reads on our two moves |
|---|---|---|---|
| Handshake | **No, and moving the other way.** Students are free, universities pay about 8,000 dollars a year, employers pay from 15,000 dollars a year, and a Job Promotions feature lets employers pay for boosted visibility in student feeds. In beta with about 30 employers, broader rollout planned for early 2026 | Not claimed | Our Eliminate row is the opposite of where the market leader is heading |
| LinkedIn | No. Promoted jobs are a core revenue line | Not claimed | Same |
| Indeed | No. Sponsored postings are the business model | Not claimed | Same |
| Jobright | Not stated anywhere public. Students pay, from free up to about 30 dollars a month, so employers are not the customer | **Not claimed.** It scans over 400,000 posts a day and matches with a mix of frontier and fine-tuned models. Public coverage says nothing about verifying that a post is real or still open | Closest to us on who pays. Nothing on verification. Scope limited to tech, education and government |
| University careers offices | Yes in principle, but they hold a small set and, as test run 01 found, their portals are closed to students at other universities | Partly, by hand, at low volume | The one place the Eliminate row already holds, and it does not scale |

## What this supports

- **The Eliminate row is a real differentiator with a date on it.** The strongest incumbent is actively building the thing we propose to remove, and plans to roll it out in early 2026. We are not removing a feature nobody wanted; we are removing the direction the category is moving in. That is a sharper position than we had before this run.
- **Nobody publicly claims verification.** Jobright's public pitch is volume and speed, 400,000 posts a day and applications filled in automatically. Volume and verification pull against each other. Combined with test run 01, where 5 of 8 candidates failed verification, the Raise row stands.

## What this works against us

- **Our university pricing assumption looks too high.** We assumed a university would pay about 18,000 dollars a year for a licence, and assumptions.md admits we had no public comparator. There now is one: Handshake charges universities about 8,000 dollars a year for the system that replaces their legacy careers software. We are proposing a second tool alongside the one they already buy, at more than twice the price of the incumbent that does more. Funnel B's revenue line does not survive that comparison unchanged.
- **Jobright is close enough to be a real problem.** Student-paid, AI matching, US only. The honest statement of our difference is narrow: we verify and we refuse employer money, they do volume and auto-apply. That is a difference in discipline, not in category. It has to show up in the product within the first ten seconds or it will not read as a difference at all.
- **This was a desk run.** Nobody on the team has used Jobright or Handshake as a student and recorded what the first screen actually shows. Until that happens, this table is reading other people's descriptions.

## Decisions taken from this run

| Decision | Reason |
|---|---|
| Keep the Eliminate row, and say the Handshake promotion rollout on stage | It turns a principle into a dated, checkable contrast |
| Mark the 18,000 dollar university price as contradicted, not just unproven, in assumptions.md and market-research.md | A public comparator at 8,000 dollars now exists |
| Add the pricing question to the careers-office script | Ask what they pay now and what a second tool would have to be worth |
| Hands-on competitor run is owed, by a team member, not by AI | A desk run cannot see what the first screen shows a student |
