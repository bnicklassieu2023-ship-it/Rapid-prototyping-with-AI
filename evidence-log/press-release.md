# Lean press-release opener

**Status:** Working Backwards draft for Group Project I, written for the United States market (rebased 1 October 2026). This describes a future product. Nothing in it is launched or validated.

**Headline:** Students stop sending hundreds of applications and start getting interviews

**Subheadline:** A shortlist that only shows jobs and internships a student can realistically get, with the reason for every match, offered through their college

BOSTON, March 2027. Today we launched a careers assistant for undergraduates that replaces endless scrolling with a short, honest list. Instead of showing everything, it shows only the roles a student actually fits, explains why each one fits, and flags which entry-level roles in their field are shrinking as AI absorbs the work.

**The problem.** Students apply to everything because they cannot tell where they have a real chance. About 30 applications go out for every role filled, and 42% of recent graduates end up underemployed. One student we interviewed had sent hundreds of applications through job platforms. He got interviews, but he was never hired through any of them. Platforms compete on how many postings they hold, and employers pay to be seen, so a student cannot tell a good fit from a paid placement.

**The solution.** We show fewer jobs on purpose. Every recommendation states why it fits and what does not fit. Nothing is promoted because an employer paid for it. Colleges offer the tool to their students, so the student pays nothing and the ranking serves the student rather than the recruiter.

"Students do not need more postings. They need to know where they have a real chance, and what their field will look like by the time they graduate." Group 3, IE University

"I had sent more applications than I can count and never got hired through a platform. I would trust something that simply never shows me jobs that do not suit me." Student interviewed in September 2026

**How it works.** A student signs in with their college account, says what they are looking for, and gets a short list with a reason for every match. They mark anything that does not fit, and the list improves. They can see which entry-level roles in their field are growing or shrinking.

**Close.** Fewer applications, better ones.

### What we are not doing

Rewriting the headline on four answers. Working Backwards is written from the future on purpose, and a press release that changes every time four people answer a form is not a vision, it is a weather report.

### What has to happen before this is presented as written

The headline claim needs the seven interviews. If the interviews also show modest application volumes, the release is rewritten around the claim that round 1 does support, which is that students cannot tell which openings are real and which they are eligible for, rather than that they are drowning in applications. That is a different and arguably sharper story, and test run 01 already supports it: 7 of 12 postings we checked failed verification and 2 were dead links.

Noted here rather than quietly fixed, so the version we present is a choice the team made with the evidence in front of it.
