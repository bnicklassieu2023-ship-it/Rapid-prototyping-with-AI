# Lean press-release opener

**Status:** Working Backwards draft for Group Project I. This describes a future product. Nothing in it is launched or validated.

**Headline:** Students stop sending hundreds of applications and start getting interviews

**Subheadline:** A shortlist that only shows jobs and internships a student can realistically get, with the reason for every match, offered through their university

MADRID, March 2027. Today we launched a careers assistant for students aged 19 to 24 that replaces endless scrolling with a short, honest list. Instead of showing everything, it shows only the roles a student actually fits, explains why each one fits, and flags which entry-level roles in their field are shrinking as AI takes over tasks.

**The problem.** Students apply to everything because they cannot tell where they have a real chance. One student we interviewed had sent hundreds of applications through job websites. He got interviews, but he was never hired through any of them. Platforms compete on how many listings they hold, and employers can pay to be seen, so a student cannot tell a good fit from a paid placement.

**The solution.** We show fewer jobs on purpose. Every recommendation states why it fits and what does not fit. Nothing is promoted because an employer paid for it. Universities offer the tool to their students, so the student pays nothing and the ranking serves the student.

"Students do not need more listings. They need to know where they have a real chance, and what their field will look like when they graduate." Group 3, IE University

"I had sent more applications than I can count and never got hired through a platform. I would trust something that simply never shows me jobs that do not suit me." Student interviewed in September 2026

**How it works.** A student signs in through their university, says what they are looking for, and gets a short list with a reason for every match. They mark anything that does not fit, and the list improves. They can see which entry-level roles in their field are growing or shrinking.

**Close.** Fewer applications, better ones.

---

**Evidence status:** the problem statement rests on one real interview and published research. The solution, the university payer and the shift monitor are hypotheses. Next proof: 7 filmed interviews and a survey, then a prototype test against our 70% first-filter threshold.
