# Survey: second test of the Job and the trust condition

**Status:** LIVE since 1 October 2026. Built in Google Forms, 11 questions, about 2 minutes, no sign-in required.

**Link sent to respondents:** https://forms.gle/mxvmrjHJAyEPbP3V6

**Where the answers land:** the Responses tab of the form, plus the sheet "Group 3 survey responses" in the team's Google Drive.

**Why we run it:** the 7 filmed interviews give depth on few people. The survey checks whether the same answers hold in a wider group, and it is built so the answers can contradict us, not only confirm us.

## The 11 questions

| # | Question | Answer type |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | How old are you? | One answer: under 19 / 19 to 21 / 22 to 24 / 25 or older |
| 2 | What are you doing right now? | One answer: bachelor / master / recently graduated and looking / working / other |
| 3 | The last time you looked for a job or internship, which platforms did you use? | Several answers: LinkedIn, InfoJobs, university careers portal, Indeed, company websites, personal contacts, ChatGPT or another AI tool, have not looked yet, other |
| 4 | Roughly how many applications did you send in that search? | One answer: 0 to 5 / 6 to 20 / 21 to 50 / 51 to 100 / more than 100 |
| 5 | Have you ever been hired through one of those platforms? | One answer: yes / no / not yet, still applying |
| 6 | What would make you trust a new website's job recommendations? Pick up to three | Several answers: every job fits my profile, it explains why each job fits, I can see where the posting came from, my data stays private, employers are verified, a friend or my university recommended it |
| 7 | How many unsuitable suggestions would it take before you stopped trusting a tool? | One answer: 1 / 2 / 3 to 5 / more than 5 / would not bother me |
| 8 | What would make you NOT use a tool like this? | Long text, optional |
| 9 | If your university offered it for free with your student login, would you use it? | One answer: yes / no / it depends |
| 10 | Would you want to be told that a role you are applying for is shrinking because of AI, and which similar roles are growing? | One answer: yes, that would change what I apply for / interesting but no change / no |
| 11 | If you are willing to do a 10-minute interview, leave your email | Short text, optional |

## What we cut from the 20-question draft, and why

Country, timing of the last search, the open trigger question, number of interviews, what they did with the last recommendation, which data they would share, who should pay, and willingness to pay. The payment and trigger questions move into the 7 filmed interviews, where follow-up questions are possible. The survey now carries only what can change a decision.

## Decision rules, fixed before any answers arrived

| Finding | Threshold | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| "Every job shown actually fits my profile" is in the top three for trust (Q6) | 60% or more | Keep accuracy as the Raise row of the ERRC grid |
| Tolerance for unsuitable matches (Q7) | 50% or more answer 1 or 2 | Raise our accuracy bar and change the threshold in experiment-card.md |
| "It explains why each job fits me" ranks above accuracy (Q6) | If it does | Put explanation back as the Raise row and report that interview 01 did not generalise |
| Would use it through the university (Q9) | 50% or more | Keep the university channel and the university as payer |
| Shift monitor would change behaviour (Q10) | Under 30% | Park the Create row as unproven rather than presenting it as a strength |
| High application counts with no hire (Q4 and Q5) | If common | The push force generalises beyond interview 01 |

## Limitations we state in the presentation

Convenience sample through our own networks, self-reported behaviour, few respondents outside IE. The survey tests whether our interview findings generalise. It does not prove demand.
