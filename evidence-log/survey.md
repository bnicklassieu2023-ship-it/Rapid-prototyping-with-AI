# Survey: second test of the Job and the trust condition

**Status:** Draft instrument, not yet launched. To be sent through the class Teams chat and our wider networks.

**Why we run it:** the 7 filmed interviews give depth on few people. The survey checks whether the same answers hold in a wider group, and it is designed so that the answers can contradict us, not only confirm us.

**What it must decide:** whether we keep the current direction (accuracy first, free to the student, university as payer, entry-level shift monitor) or change it.

## Questions

**Screening**

1. How old are you? (under 19 / 19 to 21 / 22 to 24 / 25 or older)
2. What are you doing right now? (studying a bachelor / studying a master / recently graduated, looking / working / other)
3. Where do you study or live? (Spain, other country)

**Past behaviour, not opinions**

4. When did you last look for a job or an internship? (this month / last 3 months / this year / more than a year ago / never)
5. What made you start that search? (open text, one line) [this is our open JTBD trigger]
6. Which platforms did you use? (multiple choice: LinkedIn, InfoJobs, university careers portal, Indeed, company websites, personal contacts, other)
7. Roughly how many applications did you send in that search? (0 to 5 / 6 to 20 / 21 to 50 / 51 to 100 / more than 100)
8. How many interviews did that produce? (0 / 1 to 2 / 3 to 5 / more than 5)
9. Have you ever been hired through one of those platforms? (yes / no / not yet, still applying)

**The trust condition we are betting on**

10. Think of the last time a platform recommended a job to you. What did you do? (applied / looked and ignored it / never noticed recommendations / do not remember)
11. What would make you trust a new website's job recommendations? Rank the top three: every job fits my profile / it explains why each job fits / I can see where the job came from / my data stays private / the employers are verified / a friend or my university recommended it
12. How many unsuitable suggestions would it take before you stopped trusting a tool? (1 / 2 / 3 to 5 / more than 5 / it would not bother me) [this sets our accuracy threshold]
13. What would make you NOT use a tool like this? (open text) [the most important disconfirming question]

**Data, payment and channel**

14. Which of these would you share to get better matches? (CV, grades, salary expectations, location, nothing of these)
15. Who should pay for a tool like this? (me, my university, employers, nobody, I do not know)
16. Would you pay for it yourself? (no / up to 5 euros a month / 5 to 10 / more than 10 / only if it got me interviews)
17. If your university offered it with your student login, would you use it? (yes / no / depends)

**The entry-level shift monitor, our Create bet**

18. How worried are you that AI is reducing entry-level jobs in your field? (not at all / a little / quite / very)
19. Would you want to be told that a role you are applying for is shrinking, and which similar roles are growing? (yes, that would change what I apply for / interesting but would not change my behaviour / no)

**Follow-up**

20. If you are willing to do a 10-minute interview, leave your email. (optional)

## Decision rules, set before we see the answers

| Finding | Threshold | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| "Every job fits my profile" is in the top three for trust | 60% or more | Keep accuracy as the Raise row, keep the ERRC grid |
| Tolerance for unsuitable matches | If 50% or more answer 1 or 2 | Raise our accuracy bar and say so in the experiment card |
| Explanation ranks above accuracy | If it does | Reinstate explanation as the Raise row and tell the class our interview finding did not generalise |
| Would use it through the university | 50% or more | Keep the university channel and the B2B2C payer |
| Shift monitor would change behaviour | Under 30% | Park the Create row as unproven and say so, rather than presenting it as a strength |
| Nobody would pay and nobody wants the university version | Both low | The business model, not the product, is the problem. Report it as a finding |

## Limitations we will state

Convenience sample through our own networks, self-reported behaviour, and a small number of respondents outside IE. The survey tests whether our interview findings generalise; it does not prove demand.
