# Survey: second test of the Job and the trust condition

**Status:** LIVE since 1 October 2026. Built in Google Forms, 11 questions, about 2 minutes, no sign-in required.

**Link sent to respondents:** https://forms.gle/mxvmrjHJAyEPbP3V6

**Where the answers land:** the Responses tab of the form, plus the sheet "Group 3 survey responses" in the team's Google Drive.

**Why we run it:** the 7 filmed interviews give depth on few people. The survey checks whether the same answers hold in a wider group, and it is built so the answers can contradict us, not only confirm us.

## What we cut from the 20-question draft, and why

Country, timing of the last search, the open trigger question, number of interviews, what they did with the last recommendation, which data they would share, who should pay, and willingness to pay. The payment and trigger questions move into the 7 filmed interviews, where follow-up questions are possible. The survey now carries only what can change a decision.

## Decision rules, fixed before any answers arrived

| Finding | Threshold | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| "Every job shown actually fits my profile" is in the top three for trust (Q6) | 60% or more | Keep accuracy as the Raise row of the ERRC grid |
| Tolerance for unsuitable matches (Q7) | 50% or more answer 1 or 2 | Raise our accuracy bar and change the threshold in experiment-card.md |
| "It explains why each job fits me" ranks above accuracy (Q6) | If it does | Put explanation back as the Raise row and report that interview 01 did not generalise |
| Would use it through the university (Q9) | 50% or more | Keep the university channel and the university as payer |

## Limitations we state in the presentation

Convenience sample through our own networks, self-reported behaviour, few respondents outside IE. The survey tests whether our interview findings generalise. It does not prove demand.

## Results, round 1, read on 7 October 2026

**n = 4. The survey is still open and closes 13 October 2026.**

### Why this is round 1, and what is still coming

The same 11 questions were also distributed by another team through a second link. **This section records only the responses to our own form, which stood at 4 when the results were read on 7 October 2026.** The second form's responses are expected and will be merged here as round 2. At that point every count below is replaced by a combined figure, the n is restated, and the decision rules are re-read against the larger sample.

Until then a rule we set before any data arrived applies, from [experiment-card.md](experiment-card.md): *under 20 responses by 13 October, report it as a weak sample instead of presenting percentages from a handful of people.* That stop condition has fired for round 1. So:

- Every figure below is written as a **count out of 4**, never as a percentage, and the presentation says n=4 out loud.
- The threshold rules below are **read but not actioned**. A 100% that rests on four people is not a finding, and presenting it as one is exactly what the evidence-discipline criterion penalises.
- Nothing in the ERRC grid, the funnels or the experiment card changes because of this round. Where round 1 points the same way as evidence we already had, it is recorded as consistent, not as confirmation.

## What works against us

**1. The push force did not generalise.** Our opportunity statement rests on students applying at high volume and still not getting hired, which came from interview 01. In round 1, two of four sent 5 applications or fewer, and two of four have been hired through a platform. Only one respondent sits anywhere near the Mass Applier pattern.

The honest reading has two parts and we are not allowed to use only the comfortable one. The charitable part: all four are bachelor students, three aged 21 or under, so they are earlier in the search than interview 01 and the pattern may appear later. The uncomfortable part: we built an opportunity on one student's experience and the first four people we asked do not look like him. Until the interviews say otherwise, "students apply at high volume and are not hired" is an assumption about a **segment we have not defined**, not a description of students in general.

**2. Students tolerate more bad matches than our strategy assumes.** Interview 01's condition for trust was being shown no unsuitable job at all. Nobody in round 1 chose 1 or 2. All four chose 3 to 5 or more than 5. Accuracy still ranks first as a reason to trust, but the cliff is nothing like as steep as we had been designing against. That is a direct contradiction between our two real-user sources, it is recorded rather than averaged away, and it is a question for the seven interviews.

**3. Privacy ranks last but one.** Only one of four picked "my data stays private" as a trust factor. We have just written a privacy position into [privacy.md](privacy.md). Round 1 says that position is a principle we hold, not a demand we discovered, and it should be presented that way rather than as something students asked for.

**4. One respondent asked for the opposite of our Reduce row.** "I would like a platform that centralizes all the job offers in one place." Our ERRC Reduce row reduces the breadth of listings on purpose. See [ERRC.md](ERRC.md) for how that tension is now recorded.

## Round 1 in one line

Round 1 is consistent with our trust assumption, unhelpful to our push force, and far too small to settle either. It does not change a single decision. Its real value is the four written objections and the two contradictions it surfaced, all of which become questions in the seven interviews.
