## 2. Factors the market competes on

| Factor | What alternatives assume or provide | Trade-off, contradiction or gap |
|---|---|---|
| Listing volume | More listings is better, and breadth is the headline number | Volume is what produced hundreds of applications and no hire (interview 01) |
| Ranking | Employers can pay for placement | A student cannot tell paid placement from fit |
| Explanation | Increasingly offered (Jobright match score, LinkedIn fit) | No longer differentiating. Accuracy may matter more |
| Price to the student | Free with ads, or a paid tier for serious users | Our Mass Applier will not pay before results |
| Data demanded | CV upload and profile completion before value | Willingness to share is untested in our segment |
| Future view of the role | Not offered by any alternative we found | Opportunity, and unproven demand |

## 5. Opportunity stress test, three findings most likely to change our decision

1. **Entry:** a credible entrant can copy an accurate shortlist quickly. Jobright shows the feature set is reachable. Our defence has to be the segment, the university channel and the shift monitor, not the matching itself.
2. **Substitution:** a general chatbot is free and already in students' hands. If students accept chatbot answers as good enough, our wedge narrows to accuracy that they can verify.
3. **Dependencies:** we depend on job-posting data and, for the shift monitor, on occupation data. If posting access is restricted or priced, the whole offer changes.
