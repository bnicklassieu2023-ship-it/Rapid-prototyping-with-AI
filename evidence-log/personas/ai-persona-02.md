# Persona 2: Karim, The Sceptic

**Status:** Proto-persona, v0.1. Built from secondary research and our opportunity.md. Checked against 1 real-user interview (interview-evidence.md). Not validated.

## Shared Job to Be Done

See evidence-log/jtbd.md. Find jobs or internships that fit, without hours of manual searching.

## Profile

- **Age:** 23
- **Context:** final-year Computer Science student at a private university in Madrid, international student, Spanish at B1 level
- **Looking for:** a junior data analyst role or graduate programme in an English-speaking team, before graduation

## Current behaviour

Filters listings by hand for language requirements and double-checks every source. Stopped trusting AI career advice after a chatbot gave him a job posting that did not exist.

## Goals

- Junior data role before graduation
- Clear reasons for every match
- English-friendly filter
- Control over his own data

## Pain points

- Listings do not say whether Spanish is required
- Generic AI advice he cannot verify
- Thesis and graduate-programme deadlines clash

## Motivations

Transparency, time saving, relevance

## Objections

- Will not upload his CV without knowing how the data is used
- Rejects any recommendation that does not explain itself

## Buying role and journey

- **Role:** user, decision maker and payer. Would pay about €8 to €10 per month for a few months if every option comes with reasons.
- **Journey:** finds it through Reddit, LinkedIn posts or a tech friend, tests it critically with a free trial and reads the privacy policy, pays during peak application months, recommends it only if it is accurate.
- **Sources of information:** LinkedIn, Reddit, Glassdoor, company career pages

## Quote (illustrative)

"Automate the search, but show me why you picked each job."

## Link to our riskiest assumption

Our riskiest assumption is that users will trust and use AI-generated opportunity recommendations as a first step. Karim is the persona most likely to prove it wrong, so he is critical to test.

## Evidence boundary

| Claim | Evidence status | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Many students avoid AI in their job search for ethical, skill or data reasons | SUPPORTED | NACE 2025: about 30% of non-users cite ethics, 25% lack of expertise, 15% personal data concerns |
| Privacy concerns shape paid decisions for utility apps | SUPPORTED | Öztürk 2026, Alphanumeric Journal |
| Karim's specific behaviour, language filtering and willingness to pay | HYPOTHESIS | Team interpretation, no real user yet |
| What explanation is enough to earn trust | UNKNOWN | Not tested |

## Validation gaps

- Ask real students: "What would a recommendation need to show before you trusted it enough to apply?"
- Test whether "why it fits / what does not fit" explanations raise trust

## Real-user check (interview 01, n=1)

| Claim | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Rejects any match without a reason | CHALLENGED | The interviewee named accuracy (no unsuitable jobs), not explanations, as his condition for trust |
| Distrusts new tools until they prove themselves | CONSISTENT | Trust would start only once the tool shows no unsuitable jobs |
| Won't share his CV without data clarity | NOT TESTED | Data sharing was not asked |

**Grounding update:** this contradiction stays visible. Next interviews must test whether explanations add trust on top of accurate matches, or whether accuracy alone is enough.
