# Persona 2: Karim, The Sceptic

**Status:** Proto-persona, v0.2. Rebased on the United States on 1 October 2026 when the team changed market. Built from secondary research and our opportunity.md, checked against 1 real-user interview (interview-evidence.md). Not validated. The earlier European version of this persona is in the repository history.

## Shared Job to Be Done

See evidence-log/jtbd.md. Find jobs or internships that fit, without hours of searching and mass applying.

## Profile

- **Age:** 23
- **Context:** senior computer science major at a private university, international student on an F-1 visa
- **Looking for:** a junior data or software role that is open to OPT, before graduation

## Current behaviour

Filters listings by hand for sponsorship and work-authorisation wording, and double-checks every source. Stopped trusting AI career advice after a chatbot sent him to a posting that did not exist.

## Goals

- A junior role before his status runs out
- Clear reasons for every match
- Roles that will actually consider his work authorisation
- Control over his own data

## Pain points

- Listings do not say whether they sponsor or accept OPT
- Generic AI advice he cannot verify
- Thesis deadlines collide with recruiting season

## Motivations

Transparency, time saving, relevance

## Objections

- Will not upload his resume without knowing how the data is used
- Rejects any recommendation that does not explain itself

## Buying role and journey

- **Role:** user, decision maker and payer. Would pay around 10 dollars a month for a few months if every option comes with reasons.
- **Journey:** finds it through Reddit or a friend in his major, tests it critically on the free tier and reads the privacy policy, pays during peak recruiting months, recommends it only if it is accurate.
- **Sources of information:** Reddit, LinkedIn, Glassdoor, company career pages

## Quote (illustrative)

"Automate the search, but show me why you picked each job."

## Link to our riskiest assumption

Our riskiest assumption is that users will trust and use AI-generated recommendations as a first step. Karim is the persona most likely to prove it wrong, so he is critical to test.

## Evidence boundary

| Claim | Evidence status | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Two thirds of graduating seniors avoid AI in the job search | SUPPORTED | NACE 2025: 67.1% did not use it |
| Their reasons are ethics, lack of expertise and fear of being caught | SUPPORTED | NACE 2025: 28.9%, 24.6%, 15.9% |
| Karim's visa filtering and willingness to pay | HYPOTHESIS | Team interpretation, no US user interviewed yet |
| What explanation is enough to earn trust | UNKNOWN | Not tested |

## Validation gaps

- Ask real students: "What would a recommendation need to show before you trusted it enough to apply?"
- Test whether work-authorisation filtering is the feature international students actually want first

## Real-user check (interview 01, n=1)

| Claim | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Rejects any match without a reason | CHALLENGED | The interviewee named accuracy, not explanations, as his condition for trust |
| Distrusts new tools until they prove themselves | CONSISTENT | Trust would start only once the tool shows no unsuitable jobs |
| Won't share his resume without data clarity | NOT TESTED | Data sharing was not asked |

**Grounding update:** this contradiction stays visible. Survey Q6 and Q7 separate accuracy from explanation.
