# Persona 1: Lucía, The Mass Applier

**Status:** Proto-persona, v0.2. Rebased on the United States on 1 October 2026 when the team changed market. Built from secondary research and our opportunity.md, checked against 1 real-user interview (interview-evidence.md). Not validated. The earlier European version of this persona is in the repository history.

## Shared Job to Be Done

See evidence-log/jtbd.md. Find jobs or internships that fit, without hours of searching and mass applying.

## Profile

- **Age:** 21
- **Context:** junior at a large public state university, business major, works part time, tight budget
- **Looking for:** a summer internship

## Current behaviour

Applies to almost everything on Handshake and LinkedIn. Tracks applications in a spreadsheet. Has sent about 40 applications and heard back from very few. Uses ChatGPT to write cover letters, not to decide where to apply.

## Goals

- First business or marketing internship
- Fewer, better-fitting applications
- More interviews

## Pain points

- Too many irrelevant listings
- No feedback on applications
- Cannot judge which roles are a realistic fit

## Motivations

Speed, getting interviews, low cost

## Objections

- Will not pay upfront
- Thinks Handshake plus LinkedIn is already enough
- Doubts AI understands her field

## Buying role and journey

- **Role:** user and sole decision maker. Pays nothing at first. Would consider a few dollars a month only if the free tier produces interviews.
- **Journey:** hears about it from classmates or TikTok, compares it with Handshake, uses the free tier during recruiting season, shares it in group chats if it works.
- **Sources of information:** campus group chats, TikTok, LinkedIn, the careers office newsletter

## Quote (illustrative)

"I've sent 40 applications and still don't know which ones I had a chance at."

## Evidence boundary

| Claim | Evidence status | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Students send many applications before landing a role | SUPPORTED | NACE 2025: about 30 applications per role |
| The early-career market is hard | SUPPORTED | New York Fed Q2 2026: 5.6% unemployment and 42% underemployment among recent graduates |
| A third of graduating seniors use AI in the job search | SUPPORTED | NACE 2025 Student Survey, 1,479 seniors |
| Lucía's specific behaviour, tools and 40 applications | HYPOTHESIS | Team interpretation, no US user interviewed yet |
| Willingness to upgrade to a paid tier | UNKNOWN | Not tested |

## Validation gaps

- Ask real students: "Walk me through the last internship you applied for. How did you decide to apply?"
- Test whether a fit-ranked shortlist changes where they apply

## Real-user check (interview 01, n=1)

| Claim | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Sends many applications with few results | SUPPORTED BY 1 INTERVIEW | Hundreds of applications through job platforms, some interviews, never hired through one |
| Needs to know where she has a real chance | CONSISTENT | He would trust a tool that shows no unsuitable jobs |
| Won't pay until it gets interviews | NOT TESTED | Payment was not asked |

**Grounding update:** the interviewee's behaviour is closest to this persona, though he was interviewed in Europe. The product decision (a ranked shortlist) now puts accuracy first: no unsuitable jobs.
